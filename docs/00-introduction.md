# Introduction: What This Cookbook Is

## Who this is for

This is for anyone who knows something that would make another person's week easier
and wants to write it down well. You might be a nurse, a bookkeeper, a small-diesel
owner, a parent, a reseller, a chess club helper, or someone who just figured out
a piece of software that everybody else finds confusing. You don't need to be a
writer. You need to be a translator between what you know and how a normal person
would phrase it when they're tired.

## The Asdale way, in one paragraph

Write like a friend would. Pick a topic that hurts people today, not one that
impresses experts. Scope a first edition small enough that you will actually finish
it. Draft in plain English with no show-off words. Teach in steady step-by-step
rhythms, because rhythm is what carries tired readers past their fear. Edit for
dignity and warmth, and never condescend, because struggling with a tool is not a
character flaw. Turn what you wrote into printables and small tools people can use.
Verify every claim like you were sending a reader out into weather you can't see.
Then ship: files, site, and a printable version.

## What this book contains

- **Chapters 1 through 8** walk you through the whole job, start to finish, in order.
- **Chapter-recipe cards** are one-page checklists for each chapter, useful when
  you are halfway through a project and need the shortcut, not the essay.
- **The tone checklist** is the single sheet to keep beside your keyboard.
- **The glossary** defines every term this book uses, in plain English.

## How this complements the open-book-starter-template

The open-book starter template is the scaffold: it gives you the folder layout,
the build setup, the conventions. This cookbook is the writing manual that sits
inside it. The two books are deliberately different:

- The starter answers *"what do my folders look like?"*
- This cookbook answers *"what do my sentences sound like?"*

Where there is overlap (file naming, build and verify steps) this cookbook stays at
the level of intent and habit rather than repeating the starter's commands. If you
have both, read the starter once for setup, then live inside this one.

## Three rules worth taping to the desk

1. **Respect the reader's tiredness.** Write for the version of them reading at
   10 p.m. after a long day.
2. **A claim you have not verified is a guess wearing a lab coat.** Check it or
   mark it as a guess.
3. **Ship one small true thing instead of half of a big vague one.**

## How to read this book

Straight through the first time, doing a tiny writing project as you go. For
example: a five-page manual, one printable checklist, and a two-file page on your
hub site. A thing you can finish this week. After that, the cards and the tone
checklist are the part you keep returning to.
