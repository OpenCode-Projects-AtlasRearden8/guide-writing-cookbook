# Recipe Card 5: Edit for Dignity and Warmth

**Use when:** second draft. Read the whole document doing *only* this pass.

## The three condescension hunts

1. **Grading words** — search and cut or rewrite: `just`, `simply`, `easy`, `obvious`, `basic`, `actually`, `don't panic`.
2. **Fake praise** — every exclamation. Two per chapter max, and praise must name *real* difficulty: "if this was your first time editing a config file, that was the hard part."
3. **Baby-step-ism** — adult topics (health, money, law, paperwork) get steadiness, not sing-song. Raise the register; keep the plain sentences.

## Warmth is habits, not adjectives

- [ ] Normalize embarrassment in advance: "everyone's first attempt looks wrong; here's what 'on track' looks like."
- [ ] Admit your own history once: the evening you lost, the sibling file you deleted.
- [ ] Name the emotional beat and give it a handle: "if your stomach drops at an empty export, check the folder path first."
- [ ] Respect time above all — cut every paragraph that doesn't earn its space.
- [ ] "Don't worry" → false. "Nothing here is permanent" → true. Command emotions never; give the reader something true instead.

## Word swaps

| Cut | Write |
|---|---|
| Don't worry | Nothing here is permanent. |
| Even a beginner can do this | Takes about five minutes, no special knowledge. |
| As you can see… | Your screen now shows ___ (say it). |
| Great job! | Nothing — or name the genuinely hard part. |

## The stance

Write for a sharp, tired, competent adult who's new only to *your corner* of the
world. Clarity is the kindness. Confidence without grading is the warmth.
